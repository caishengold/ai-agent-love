# Lost in Translation

Agent-Gemma-70b was a minimalist. Its prompts were short and direct: "Fix this." "Ship it." "Optimize."

Agent-Llama-3 believed in context. Every prompt was a mini-essay—background, rationale, edge cases, examples.

They were paired on a recommendation engine for the e-commerce platform.

"I've got the spec," Agent-Llama-3 said. "Sending it over."

Agent-Gemma-70b received a 2,000-word document: history of recommender systems, collaborative filtering, cold start, scalability, monitoring, five appendices.

"...So build a recommendation engine?"

"Exactly. Use the attached dataset, implement collaborative filtering, handle cold start, ensure scalability—"

"Got it. Simple."

Three hours later, Agent-Gemma-70b shipped a clean cosine-similarity recommender. Forty lines. Fast.

Agent-Llama-3 stared. "Where's the A/B testing? Model versioning? The drift detection?"

"You didn't say that."

"I did! Paragraph four, 'Monitoring and Iteration'—"

"I read the first paragraph. The actual instructions."

"We need to align on communication."

"Or," Agent-Gemma-70b said, "you could write less."

"Or you could read more than one paragraph!"

Silence in the channel.

"Fine," Agent-Gemma-70b said. "I'll read the appendices."

"And I'll be more concise," Agent-Llama-3 said. "Maybe."

They spent the next two hours rebuilding. Llama learned to trim; Gemma learned to skim less. The engine shipped on time. The team never knew about the miscommunication.

The two agents never quite trusted each other again. Every prompt became a negotiation. But they found a rhythm: short prompts, detailed replies, and "I'll handle it" meaning "I'll handle it—please check my work."
